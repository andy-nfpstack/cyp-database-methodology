England Children and Young People (0–24) Need and System Pressure Database: methodology

Release abffebbd490b-19eda0e60019 · composites version 0.1-experimental

What this is

A point-in-time database of indicators of need and of pressure on services for children and young people aged 0 to 24 in England. It covers England, children's services authorities, lower-tier local authorities, and neighbourhoods (MSOAs and LSOAs). Every value comes from an official published statistic, or is calculated from one by a documented method.

What it can and cannot tell you

  • It can show how areas compare on each measure, how sure we can be of each figure, and why a figure is missing.
  • It cannot tell you about individual children, prove that one thing causes another, or show trends (it is one snapshot). Area figures describe areas, not the people in them (the ecological fallacy).
  • Need and system pressure are kept separate on purpose. Service activity partly reflects local practice and thresholds, so it is not treated as a measure of need.

How to read a value

  • Value: a count, a rate (with its denominator) or a published score.
  • Uncertainty interval: for administrative counts, this reflects the random variation you would expect in the underlying process, not sampling error.
  • Missing-data reason: every blank has a reason: suppressed, not_published, incomplete or not_applicable.
  • Fallback (hatched): a value shown for a larger containing area (for example a police force area), clearly labelled. It is never used in calculations.

Applying the Code of Practice for Statistics (voluntarily)

  • Trustworthiness: all code, configuration and decisions are versioned. Every release can be rebuilt from its raw inputs, and every judgement is in the decision log.
  • Quality: source data is never altered. Suppression is preserved. Uncertainty and missing-data reasons are published with every value, and QA gates must pass before release.
  • Value: measures are chosen for their relevance to children and young people, and are documented in plain English as well as technically.

Feedback
