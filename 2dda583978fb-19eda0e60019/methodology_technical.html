Technical methodology

Release 2dda583978fb-19eda0e60019. Every parameter below is generated from the build configuration.

1. Principles

Source data is never altered. Values flow one way through layers L0 (raw) → L1 (staged) → L2 (harmonised, observed only) → L3 (derived) → L4 (release). L5 composites read the snapshot's L2–L3 values read-only and never change L0–L4 (D-0086). Observed and modelled values never share a table. Counts move between geographies, never rates. A value is never distributed from an area to the areas it contains. Published figures take precedence over aggregated ones. Suppression is preserved and nothing is imputed.

2. Geography

The hierarchy is LSOA21 → MSOA21 → LAD (April 2025) → UTLA (April 2025) → England. Nesting is proven in two ways. First, every Output Area in an LSOA or MSOA must map to the same LAD. Second, each LSOA and MSOA polygon must lie at least 99.9% by area inside its LAD, using full-resolution (BFC) boundaries. Generalised boundaries are used for display only.

Other geographies (Sub-ICB, ICB, youth justice services, police force areas, wards) are related to the hierarchy as coterminous (relabelled), nests_within (summed), contains (no conversion; shown as a labelled fallback) or overlaps (apportioned in L3 on the matching age-band population, using GP-registered population for health data, and flagged unsuitable for spatial analysis).

3. Sources and disclosure control

Each source's statistical disclosure control (SDC) determines how closely sums of its cells can be expected to match its published totals. The profile below is taken from each publisher's own data guidance, read when the file was inspected, and confirmed against the published totals where these exist. A sum is "within tolerance" when |sum − published| ≤ absolute tolerance + relative tolerance × published. Tolerances were fixed before the comparison and not tuned to it (except DWP Stat-Xplore, F2–F4, set from observed differences in Phase 2). A difference outside tolerance is reported, never corrected: the published figure is always the value used.

6. Rates, denominators and confidence intervals

A rate is the numerator divided by the denominator, multiplied by the indicator's multiplier. The denominator must match the numerator's area, age band, sex and ethnicity classification. Stock measures use the population estimate closest to the reference date, and flow measures use the mid-period population. The denominator date must be within max_denominator_gap_days of the numerator period (default 366 days).

Confidence intervals:

  • Wilson score: person-based proportions and prevalences.
  • Byar's approximation to the exact Poisson: event counts that can repeat (OHID/PHE methods).
  • Published: the publisher's own interval.
  • None: model-based, survey-calibrated and index values, and published rates without intervals.

For full administrative counts, an interval reflects random variation in the underlying process, not sampling error.

A rate is flagged low_reliability when its numerator is below the indicator's threshold (default 10) or its denominator is below its threshold (default 50).

7. Small-area smoothing

Rates for small areas are also given as empirical Bayes estimates (Marshall's global method, shrinking toward the England rate). Smoothed rates never replace the crude rate. Where the resident population is known to misrepresent exposure (for example crime in town centres), the rate is flagged low_validity_denominator.

8. Comparability

Each indicator is given an England percentile (0–100, ties averaged, missing values excluded) and a z-score, calculated separately at each level from native and L2 values only. Fallback rows are excluded. Polarity is recorded as metadata, and signs are never flipped.

9. Indices of Deprivation

Published IoD 2025 LSOA values and the published LA summary measures are loaded as observed values. For MSOAs and custom regions, MHCLG's method is used: the population-weighted average score and the share of population in the most deprived national decile. The method is validated by recomputing the published LAD averages. Ranks and deciles are never averaged.

10. Limitations

  • Period mismatches: sources range from Census 2021 to 2025. Each value carries its own period.
  • Ecological fallacy: area values describe areas, not individuals.
  • Modifiable areal unit problem: patterns can change with the choice of boundaries.
  • Practice effects: service measures reflect local thresholds and recording practice.
  • Interval coverage: confidence intervals describe random variation only; they do not cover bias from recording practice, coverage gaps, survey calibration or the period mismatch between numerator and denominator.
  • Point in time: this is one snapshot (reference date 31 March 2025), not a time series; differences between areas are not trends.
  • Small numbers: use the intervals and smoothed rates.
